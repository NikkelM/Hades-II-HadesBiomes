1. Rotate hue
   This turns the gold into red while preserving the shape, the alpha and the greyscale pixels exactly.

2. Darken the lower (originally orange) end of the gradient
   Hue rotation preserves Value, but the gold gradient's top-to-bottom separation came mostly from luminance.
   #FFD000 has luminance 0.664 and #FF9B00 has 0.447, a ratio of 0.674.
   After rotation the reds sit at 0.239 and 0.213, a ratio of 0.891, so the two halves visually collapse together.
   The fix is a ramp keyed to each pixel's ORIGINAL hue: the yellow end is left alone and the orange end is
   multiplied down in Value, which restores (and here slightly exceeds) the original separation.

Per-icon parameters:

  icon                        hue shift   lower-end Value multiplier ("floor")
  WantsToTalk                   -36 deg   0.80
  WantsToTalk_Important         -40 deg   0.74

The two hue shifts differ because the Important icon's gold sits at a higher hue (mean 51.9 deg vs 43.5 deg).
-40 on the Important icon lands its dominant colour on #FF3600, the same hue as the base icon at -36.
Using -36 on both would leave the Important icon visibly more orange than the base one.

The floors were chosen by eye from a comparison sheet, not by formula.
For reference, floor 0.86 reproduces the gold original's separation exactly on the base icon.


Reproducing it (GIMP, approximate)
----------------------------------
The hue step is exact in GIMP. The ramp is not, because it is keyed to per-pixel original hue.

  1. Open the H1 original.
  2. Colors > Hue-Saturation, select Master, set Hue to -36 (base) or -40 (Important), click OK.
     This is safe. Greyscale pixels are hue-invariant, so the bubble and outline cannot change.
  3. For the darkening, do NOT use Lightness or Saturation on Master.
     Those are not hue-invariant and WILL darken the white bubble (measured: up to 32/255 shift).
     Switch to the R (red) range tab first, then reduce Lightness by roughly 12 to 15.
     This approximates the ramp but applies it flat across the mark rather than graded.
